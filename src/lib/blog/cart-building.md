I often like to work alone.
I like to do the task or project in solitude.
Only after it is done, I present my shiny implementation to the customer.
This part of my nature was very well suited to school, especially math and science.
Given a problem, I could steal away to my study.
I could lose myself, dissolve myself, fully reconstruct myself in a problem.
This is safe.
I can be wrong alone.
I can take my time.
I only have to contend with one perspective.
I can doing it _perfectly_.

I recently wrapped a software project.
Without talking to the customer of the software, I worked for 6 months on what I thought the problems were.
To no one's surprise but my own, many of the things that I found weren't on anyone's radar.
One customer didn't even know they were problems.
In trying to doing to doing it perfectly with no help, I succeeded in doing plenty of work that no asked for or needed.

More recently, I have been writing about marrying my girlfriend.
I find marriage to be a big and important decision.
As such, I want it to be very considered.
So, I've spent the past month writing various prenuptial essays.
After I had written a few of them, I felt angry with her for being less engaged with what I had been writing.
But after talking with her about it, what avenues did I give her for engaging with the work.
True to habit I stole away to my study to make sure that my girlfriend and I should get married.

What's more, wouldn't you know that she felt like I was procrastinating the proposal.
In her defense, I think I was toward the end.
But far and away from a legal argument about how I technically wasn't putting it off, I certainly secluded myself with a singular self-derived view of a task and worked through it asking for now input.
This resulted in my most important customer being underserved.

So, going forward, I need to bring the proverbial car out from behind closed doors.
Starting a project is very different from getting a homework assignment.
A homework assignment is fully done before it is given to you.
The answer already exists.
To make something new, you have to go into uncharted territory.
I want to stop trying to pridefully do that alone.
